CALCULATION TRANSPARENCY

How Stockerboard calculates and presents data

Stockerboard believes financial information is more useful when users understand where a number comes from, what it means, and what its limitations are.

Effective: October 2026 • Stockerboard Trust & Transparency Architecture

Core Product Principle

Stockerboard explains. Stockerboard does not predict.

All financial ratios, valuation indicators, and screening conditions presented on Stockerboard are algorithmic and mathematical. They are designed to help investors contextualize public information systematically. Inclusion of a company in any list, table, or screen does not constitute a recommendation, endorsement, or prediction of future returns.

Key Metrics & How They Are Derived

Market Capitalization Valuation Scale

What it means: The total aggregate market value of a company's outstanding equity shares as settled on the exchange.

Market Capitalization = Last Traded Price (CMP) × Total Certified Paid-up Equity Shares

Stockerboard rule: Formatted in Indian Crores (₹ Cr). Used to classify companies by capital scale (Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Micro Cap).

Important to know: Computed on an End-of-Day (EOD) closing settlement basis. Outstanding share counts are refreshed based on official regulatory corporate filings and adjust periodically during corporate actions.

Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E) Valuation Multiple

What it means: Shows how much the market is currently paying for each rupee of annual earnings generated by the business.

P/E Ratio = Closing Share Price / Diluted Earnings Per Share (TTM EPS)

Stockerboard rule: Compares trailing twelve-month earnings with the latest closing price. Companies with negative earnings or unavailable P/E values are excluded from positive valuation filters and presented as — Not available.

Important to know: A low P/E does not automatically mean a stock is inexpensive. Cyclical windfalls, one-time extraordinary gains, or accounting adjustments can artificially distort trailing earnings.

Return on Equity (ROE) Capital Efficiency

What it means: Return on Equity compares company profit with shareholders' equity, measuring how efficiently management reinvests equity capital.

ROE (%) = (Net Profit After Tax / Average Shareholder Net Worth) × 100

Stockerboard rule: Evaluates corporate profitability and equity compounding ability across consecutive fiscal periods using certified balance sheet statements.

Important to know: A high ROE should not be interpreted alone. Debt, one-time gains, industry structure, and capital intensity can affect the ratio. ROE should always be cross-referenced with total borrowings and Return on Capital Employed (ROCE).

52-Week High Distance Price Positioning

What it means: The percentage distance between the most recent eligible closing market price and the company's verified 365-day high price.

52-Week High Distance (%) = ((Current Price - 52-Week High) / 52-Week High) × 100

Stockerboard rule: Expressed as a negative percentage relative to the 52-week peak (for example, -2.4% means trading 2.4% below its recorded peak; 0.0% means trading at its yearly peak).

Important to know: Measures price location, not intrinsic valuation. Proximity to historical highs or lows is descriptive and does not indicate future direction.

Named Screen Rules & Public Criteria

Stockerboard provides pre-configured quantitative screens. Each filter applies a transparent, objective mathematical rule to the active coverage universe.

ROE ≥ 15% Screen Preset

What it means: Return on Equity compares company profit with shareholders' equity.

Stockerboard rule: A company appears in this screen when its available certified ROE is at least 15% (ROE ≥ 15.0%).

Important to know: A high ROE should not be interpreted alone. Debt, one-time gains, industry structure, and capital intensity can affect the ratio. Companies with null or unreported ROE values are excluded from this screen.

P/E ≤ 25 Screen Preset

What it means: Price-to-Earnings compares the market price with earnings attributable to each share.

Stockerboard rule: A company qualifies when P/E is greater than 0 and P/E is less than or equal to 25. Companies with negative earnings or unavailable P/E values are excluded from this screen.

Important to know: A low P/E does not automatically mean a stock is inexpensive. Depressed valuations often reflect market expectations of declining future earnings or cyclical challenges.

At / Very Close to 52-Week High Screen Preset

What it means: Identifies companies whose closing price is trading right at or virtually at their highest level of the past 52 weeks.

Stockerboard rule: The closing price is within approximately 0.5% of the stored 52-week high (distance from 52-week high is greater than or equal to -0.5%).

Important to know: Proximity to historical price highs is a descriptive location metric, not a forecast or buying signal. Requires valid rolling 365-day historical trading records.

Near 52-Week High Screen Preset

What it means: Identifies companies trading in a tight corridor just below their 52-week high.

Stockerboard rule: The closing price is within 3% of the stored 52-week high, subject to Stockerboard's current screening boundary (distance between -3.0% and -0.05% below the high).

Important to know: Historical price proximity does not indicate whether a stock will break out or pull back. Requires valid rolling 365-day price history.

Understanding Screen Overlap: Stockerboard screens are independent quantitative filters rather than mutually exclusive categories. A company can therefore satisfy more than one screen at the same time. For example, a company trading between approximately 0.5% and 0.05% below its recorded 52-week high can satisfy both the Near 52-week High and At 52-week High conditions. In drawer summaries, proximity within 0.5% takes display precedence as At 52-week High.

Technical Pattern Classifications

In Pro research and advanced views, Stockerboard provides quantitative pattern-matching tags (such as Stage 2 Uptrend, VCP base forming, Pocket Pivot, and Volume Surge).

These conditions are strictly quantitative screening conditions and pattern classifications derived from historical data (such as moving average slopes and volume standard deviations relative to trailing 50-day baselines). They represent analytical filters rather than trade signals, recommendations, opportunities, expected winners, or guaranteed setups.

Missing Data & Temporal As-of Dates

Different data points necessarily have different origin dates:

  • Market Quotes: Derived from the latest eligible trading session (official NSE EOD settlement).
  • Financial Ratios: Derived from the latest certified annual or quarterly XBRL regulatory filing released by the company.

When a metric is missing, unfiled, or non-calculable (e.g., P/E on a loss-making firm), Stockerboard renders — Not available. Stockerboard strictly avoids interpolating or guessing missing numbers.
